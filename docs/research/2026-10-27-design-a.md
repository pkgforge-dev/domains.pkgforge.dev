## 0. The design in one page

**What it is.** A public dataset plus a thin read API. Ingesters turn many feeds into small immutable observation segments (a name, when each source saw it, and which source). Compaction merges the segments into a log-structured, hash-partitioned index of the names and of the registrable domains. Both live as OCI artifacts in GHCR. Cloudflare Workers read GHCR (Range reads on blobs, which GHCR serves from Azure Blob Storage) and cache in R2 (at most 5 GB) and the Cache API.

**Decisions, each backed by evidence below.**

1. **GitHub Actions is the only writer. Workers only read.** Actions is free for public repositories on 4 vCPU / 16 GB runners [R: ghdocs/billing-actions.md, hosted-ref.md]. GHCR storage and bandwidth are free, with at least one month of notice before any change [R: ghdocs/packages-billing.md]. No Cloudflare credential sits in GitHub and no GitHub credential sits in Cloudflare. R2 is filled only by Workers, as a read-through cache and a cron warmer.
2. **No source is complete, so there is no "primary" source.** On the same 5 minutes of 2026-10-26, THC's day dump held only 39.8% of the names OpenINTEL's Kafka stream saw [M8], and that stream carried no Let's Encrypt or Geomys log in its sources [M6]. THC held 97.7% to 98.1% of the Let's Encrypt names [M9]. Every record therefore carries per-source first and last seen, and the service publishes a daily cross-source coverage table.
3. **Two sources nobody on the list had are the backbone of "newly registered".**
   - OpenINTEL's Kafka broker answers anonymously on plain port 9092 [M6]. Its `certstream_domains` topic carries 72.7 M messages in 24 h with 27 h of replayable retention. Its `newly_registered_domain` topic carries 422,192 messages in 24 h with about 11.6 days of retention. Offsets make the feed replayable, so no held WebSocket is needed.
   - Let's Encrypt, Geomys and IPng "names tiles" are plain HTTPS: about 52 M entries a day at 26 gzip bytes per entry, about 1.4 GB a day [M7, M10].
4. **"Newly registered" is never a boolean.** The API returns `registered: {basis, confidence, sources[]}`, with evidence tiers from zone and RDAP down to CT first sight. "Newly seen" is a separate, always-available property, defined against a stated corpus horizon.
5. **THC's query types are served in two ways.** Names and subdomains come from our own index. Reverse IP and CNAME targets are brokered from THC's live API with stale-serve and clearly labelled, because only THC has that data free. An OpenINTEL measurement-stream adapter and the THC monthly dump are the planned second and third sources for them.
6. **Few GHCR packages, many blobs.** GHCR cannot make a package public by API [R: ma-clone/README.md, "Add a target"]. The design uses five packages and packs many shards into a few multi-hundred-MB blobs, each with a footer directory, read by HTTP Range.
7. **Phase 0 measures the unmeasured before any production work.** The list is in §11.

## 1. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**
- Publish CT-derived names and newly seen or newly registered domains, from many prepublished feeds plus our own CT reading, under licence 0BSD [operator ruling].
- Any source can disappear and the service keeps answering from the others. Every answer states which sources it used and which it could not reach.
- Accuracy by definition. "Seen", "registered" and "new" are defined. Each record carries first and last seen per source, with a precision marker (second or day).
- THC parity: reverse IP to domains, subdomains of a domain, CNAME targets, CSV downloads, daily CT dumps.
- Fine-grained queries of our own: new or newly registered registrable domains in the last 1 h, 6 h, 12 h, 1 d or N days, for any suffix (`com.np`, `co.uk`, `xyz`) or parent domain, with first-seen and last-seen per name and per source.
- Storage: R2 at most 5 GB as a cache, the majority in GHCR.
- A clean slot for our own VPS ingester later, with no change to anything downstream.

**Non-goals (today)**
- No VPS. No full read of all CT logs (about 510 GB a day [R: ct.md §4]).
- No heavy regex pipelines. Name normalisation is a byte-class scan plus a Public Suffix List trie.
- No consumer keys, accounts or quotas (the web.pkgforge.dev convention). Cost is bounded by caps on window and page size, never by refusing a caller.
- No DNS resolution at scale. Forward DNS comes only from existing streams.
- No legal or terms-of-service analysis. The source table states each source's stated licence as a fact. Every record carries a source bitmask, so excluding a source from published outputs is mechanical (§12).

- A manifest GET took 0.531 s (1,609 B).
- The blob 307 step took 0.40 to 0.51 s.
- The signed URL is on `pkg-containers.githubusercontent.com`, served by `Windows-Azure-Blob/1.0`, with `Accept-Ranges: bytes`. It expired about 7 minutes after issue (`se=05:30:00Z` against issue at 05:23Z).

## 4. Definitions and data model

### 4.1 Normalisation (applied by every adapter, with no regex)
- Lowercase ASCII, IDNA A-label (punycode), no trailing dot (Zonestream sends one), no leading `*.`. The wildcard is kept as a flag `wild`.
- Reject names with a byte outside `[a-z0-9_.-]` after IDNA, a label over 63 bytes, a name over 253, or no dot. (THC's 38.5 M lines on 10-26 had 0 rejects [M5].)
- Suffix tables come from the pinned PSL. `suffix` is the ICANN-section public suffix; `reg` (the registrable domain) is the suffix plus one label. `site` uses the full list, private section included, so tenant hosts (`x.github.io`) do not look like one registrant. My measurement on 10-26: 11.16 M `reg` against 14.16 M `site` [M5].
- OpenINTEL's `sld_list` is recomputed by us from the PSL. Disagreements go to the validation report (§9).

### 4.2 Records

**Observation** (the unit of ingestion, never edited): `name`, `source`, `t_first`, `t_last`, `n`, `wild`, plus the segment's `precision` and `basis`. `t_*` are the source's own times: `Timestamp` for tiles, `seen` for Kafka, the dump date for THC.

**Name record** (merged index): `name`; derived `reg`, `suffix`, `site`; `first_seen` and `last_seen` (global minimum and maximum over sources); and per source present `(first, last)`. The source set is a bitmask over a registered id table (at most 64). There are no counts, so merges stay idempotent.

**Registrable-domain record**: `reg`, `suffix`; per-source `(first, last)`; `name_count_bucket`; and `evidence[]` for registration:
`{tier, basis, source, at, confidence}`.

| Tier | Basis | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | `zone` or `rdap` | authoritative: first appearance in a registry zone, a zone-diff add, or an RDAP registration event |
| T2 | `nrd-list` or `nrd-ct+rdap(confidence)` | a third party claims it |
| T3 | `ct-first` | first certificate seen in our corpus (a proxy, not a registration) |

### 4.3 Definitions
- **Newly seen** (name or registrable domain): no source in our corpus observed it before `t`. It is relative to `corpus_since`, which the API returns. While the corpus is younger than 90 days, `new` answers carry `confidence: "warming"`. A baseline seed (the THC dumps back to 2026-09-01, plus a Common Crawl domain-rank set, 135 M domains, as a "pre-existing" marker) reduces false novelty at start.
- **Newly registered**: a claim about a registry, never inferred from "newly seen" alone. The API's `basis` filter selects the strictness: `any`, `registered` (T1 or T2), `zone`, `rdap`, `nrd-list`, `ct`.
- **`first_seen` precision** is part of the record: `second` for tiles and Kafka, `day` for THC. A day-precision value is never presented as a time.
- **`last_seen`** is the latest time any source observed the name. It is not a liveness claim.

## 5. Ingestion pipeline

### 5.1 Where each step runs

| Step | Runs on | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| THC dump, Kafka `certstream_domains`, tiles, NRD lists, zone diffs | **GitHub Actions** (cron), Go tool `dtool` | free for public repositories; 4 vCPU; 6 h jobs; Kafka with offsets needs a real client (snappy-compressed batches) |
| Merge and compaction | Actions matrix (≤256 jobs, ≤20 at once) | parallel by partition range; each job reads Range slices from GHCR, writes a new slice |
| Root-manifest promotion | Actions, one job, `main` only | forward-only pointer, as in mirror-archlinux's promote |
| Serving, caching, warming R2 | Workers (cron, 1 minute) | reads only, no secrets |
| Health, freshness | Worker cron plus a self-reporting status artifact | independent of Actions |
| Workflow-keepalive and overdue dispatch | Actions workflows start each other, as in mirror-archlinux (`dispatch-overdue`, `keep-schedules-enabled`) | GitHub drops or delays schedules and disables them after 60 days without activity [R: events-that-trigger-workflows] |

No Containers, Sandboxes or Durable Objects are used, and no held WebSocket. Zonestream's WebSocket is only a documented fallback if Kafka disappears: a job holding it for up to 55 minutes each hour.

Rejected: tiles on a 1-minute cron Worker. It would give fresher data and a hot buffer, but needs a second runtime and R2 writes from a place that is not the cache. It stays optional if "last 15 minutes" becomes a requirement (Phase 3). Actions every 20 minutes already meets 1 h, 6 h and 12 h windows.

### 5.2 Schedules (UTC, off the top of the hour because GitHub delays it)

| Workflow | Schedule | Job | Estimated runner time [E] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ingest-thc` | `20 0 * * *`, plus `20 * * * *` retry until the day's file exists | download to a pipe, normalise, write one segment | 5 to 10 min (my Node run took 527 s for hashing alone on a desktop; a Go tool with a trie should take a fraction. **Measure in Phase 0**) |

Estimated total: about 6 to 7 runner-hours a day [E], free on public repositories, inside the 20-job concurrency the organisation shares with mirror-archlinux and every other repository. Phase 0 measures the real number.

### 5.3 Idempotence, dedupe, cursors
- **Segment identity** is `(source, window, cursor_before, cursor_after)`. A segment tag is written once (mirror-archlinux's write-once tag rule). A re-run with the same inputs produces identical bytes (sorted, fixed compression parameters), so a repeat is a no-op.
- **Cursors** live in a tiny `state` artifact: the Kafka offset per topic, the next tile index per log (with the log's tree size at last read), the last THC day, ETags. A cursor is advanced only in the same job that pushed the segment, and the state push is guarded as mirror-archlinux's index writer is (read the live tag, push only if it is the one this plan read).
- **Dedupe** is a property of the merge. Records merge by key `name` with `min(first)`, `max(last)`, union of sources. It is commutative, associative and idempotent, so segment order and retries do not matter. Counts are not stored in merged tiers for exactly this reason.
- **Within one source**, a name is written once per segment (sorted unique). The 5-minute Kafka window had 194,303 messages and 239,171 distinct names [M8], and tiles had about 1.3 names per entry [M10], so segment size is driven by distinct names, not messages.
- **Reference data** (PSL, CT log list, IANA) is pinned by sha256 in `ref`. A new PSL version triggers a `reg`/`suffix` recompute at the next compaction.

### 5.4 Backfill

| Source | Backfill reach |
| --- | --- |
| THC | 54 days (Sept 1 to Oct 26) from the Hugging Face bucket: 35.8 GB, matrix of 20 jobs, one day each (about 10 min each [E]) |
| Kafka `certstream_domains` | 27 h only [M6] |
| Kafka NRD topics | about 11 days only |
| Tiles | any time since a shard began, by binary search on entry timestamps (21 probes measured [M10]); 7 days of Sycamore2027h1 alone is about 110 M entries, about 2.9 GB gz. Limit the backfill to 7 days |
| NRD lists | their window (10 to 60 days) |

Because the corpus horizon is the earliest backfilled day, early `new` answers are labelled `warming`.

### 5.5 Plugging in a new source, or our own VPS ingester
A source is a directory `adapters/<id>/` with a manifest (`source` id and bit, `cadence`, `precision`, `basis`, `trust` tier, expected rate band) and an emitter. The only contract is the segment format (§6.2). The merge job treats every `obs/<source>` tag uniformly. Adding a source is: allocate a bit in `sources.json`, add the adapter, add a failure-mode row. Nothing downstream changes.

The VPS ingester later is just another emitter, `adapters/vps-ct/`, pushing the same segments. Per mirror-archlinux's rule [R: design.md "Running outside GitHub Actions"], the package must be created and linked by a workflow first. The VPS then uses a dedicated account with write access to that one package, never the maintainer's token. The merge job validates every segment (schema, sortedness, rate against the band, sha256 in annotations) before it counts. A VPS ingester that reads whole CT logs would replace the tile adapter without changing the index, and would only need a larger names set.

## 6. Storage layout

### 6.1 GHCR packages (five; each made public once by a person)
Namespace `ghcr.io/pkgforge-dev/domains.pkgforge.dev/` (the repository's name, as mirror-archlinux does).

| Package | Holds | Tags |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `obs` | immutable observation segments | `<source>-<UTC window>-<seq>`, write-once |
| `index` | tier packs and the **root manifest** | `root` (moves forward only), `root-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM` (immutable history), `b-…`, `w-…`, `d-…`, `h-…` for tiers |
| `dumps` | daily bulk downloads | `names-YYYY-MM-DD`, `new-YYYY-MM-DD`, `names-by-source-<source>-YYYY-MM-DD` |
| `state` | cursors | `state` plus `state-YYYY-MM-DD` snapshots |
| `ref` | PSL, CT log list, IANA, the baseline seed, `sources.json` | by sha256 |

A manifest is addressed by digest from the root. Only the root and the day tags are ever looked up by tag. This keeps tag counts low (about 125 segment tags a day, about 46,000 a year [E]) and the manual "make public" steps to five.

### 6.2 Formats

**Segment (`DSEG1`, `application/vnd.pkgforge.domains.obs.v1`)**: one layer `obs.tsv.zst`, sorted by name: `name`, `t_first`, `t_last`, `n`, `flags`. Annotations: `dev.pkgforge.domains.source`, `.window.start`, `.window.end`, `.cursor.before`, `.cursor.after`, `.count`, `.sha256`, `.precision`, `.schema`, and the OCI `source`, `title`, `created`.

**Pack (`DPACK1`)**, the tier unit. One blob holds many partitions, sorted and front-coded, in about 64 KB blocks, each block compressed with a format the Worker can decode natively (see the open question in §13 on `DecompressionStream`). Each partition has a footer: a sparse block index of first key, offset and length, 2 KB for about 100 blocks. Layout: `[partition 0: block… footer] [partition 1…] …`, with blobs targeting 1 to 2 GB, well under the 10 GB layer cap and a 10-minute upload at the mirror's measured 34 MB/s. A record is: shared-prefix length, suffix length, suffix bytes, `first_seen` as a varint, `last_seen − first_seen`, source bitmask, and per-source `(first, last)` deltas.

**Root manifest** (`index:root`, `application/vnd.pkgforge.domains.root.v1`), a few KB with layers:
- `directory.bin`: per tier, per partition, `(pack index, offset, length)`. 4,096 partitions is about 53 KB per tier.
- `tiers.json`: tier list, digests, ranges, `as_of`, `corpus_since`, counts.
- `sources.json`: source ids, bits, status, last good time.
- `coverage.json`: the cross-source table (§9).
- Annotations: schema version, run id, previous root digest (a chain for rollback).

The root is promoted forward only (as mirror-archlinux's `latest`), and clients tolerate unknown fields.

### 6.3 Index structure (what is stored, and why it answers each query)

| Structure | Key | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **`names` tiers** (LSM: monthly base `b-`, weekly `w-`, daily `d-`, hourly `h-`) | partition `xxh3(reg) mod 4096`, then sorted by `(reg, name)` | `subdomains`, `name`, `domain` |
| **`regs` table** | partition by `reg`; one record per registrable domain with per-source times and `evidence[]` | `domain`, `lookup`, `new` evidence |
| **`new-reg` day files** | per UTC day (and per hour for the last 48 h); sorted by `(suffix key, reg)` where the suffix key is the reversed-label suffix (`np.com\0name`), so `.com.np` is one contiguous range; each record has `first_seen`, `basis`, source bits | `/v1/new` for registrables |
| **`new-name` day files** | the same layout for names first seen that day; kept 30 days | `/v1/new?kind=fqdn` for a suffix or parent |
| **`dumps`** | THC-compatible and richer files | bulk download |

Sizing estimates [E; Phase 0 step 3 replaces them with a measured 54-day build]:
- Names seen per year are 1.6 to 4.2 B on THC's own estimate [R: ct.md]. My measurement: 125.5 M name-days over four days with 115.0 M distinct, and 85.8% of day 4 new against the prior three days, so the true total depends on the renewal tail beyond four days.
- At about 10 to 14 bytes a name (front-coded name, first and last, bitmask), the `names` index with 400-day retention (by last seen) is about **20 to 55 GB**. Names unseen for 400 days leave the `names` index but stay in `obs` and in the `regs` table.
- `regs`: about 100 to 250 M registrable domains at about 14 B is about **1.4 to 3.5 GB**.
- `new-reg` day files: first-ever-seen registrables are about 0.4 to 0.8 M a day (OpenINTEL's NRD topic is 422k a day [M6]; hagezi's 396k [M12]); 15 to 30 MB a day.
- `new-name` day files: about 28 M names a day [M5] at about 9 B is about 250 MB a day; 30 days is about 7.5 GB.
- `obs` segments: Kafka about 0.4 GB, tiles about 0.5 GB, THC about 0.32 GB, a day [E]. About 450 GB a year, no deletion in v1. mirror-archlinux already holds 368.5 GiB in one organisation [R: measurements.md].
- `dumps`: union dump about 350 to 450 MB a day with zstd; 30 days about 12 GB [E].

### 6.4 R2 (at most 5 GB)
R2 is a cache, filled only by Workers, with a bucket lifecycle rule that expires objects after 7 days.

| Use | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| Root manifest, directory and `sources.json` copies | 30 MB |
| Partition footers of the current tiers (4,096 × about 2 KB × up to 4 tiers) | 40 MB |
| Hot data: the `h-` tier and the last 48 h of `new-*` files, copied from GHCR after each promotion | about 1 GB |
| Read-through cache of blocks (`blk/<digest>/<off>-<len>`, immutable) | about 3.5 GB, LRU by access |
| Worker state (THC passthrough cache, status) | 50 MB |
| Reserve | 0.4 GB |

Cache API (per colo) sits in front of R2. GHCR is the only copy of the truth.

### 6.5 Why GHCR serves concurrency well (evidence)
Anonymous reads need no token (`Bearer QQ==` works [R: measurements.md]); 30 simultaneous manifest GETs all returned 200; blob bytes come from Azure Blob signed URLs [M11], not from GHCR's API. The only rate limit measured is on writes (2,000 requests a minute per token), which the design sidesteps by writing few large blobs.

## 7. Query API (Workers, `domains.pkgforge.dev`)

A single Worker `web-domains`, reusing forge's GHCR client. No keys. All routes are GET unless stated, JSON by default, `format=ndjson|csv|txt` supported on list routes.

### 7.1 THC parity

| THC | Ours | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `POST /lookup` (`ip_address`, `tld[]`, `apex_domain`, `page_state`, `limit`) → `domains[{domain, apex_domain, tld, organization, country, city, asn}]` | `GET /v1/ip/{ip-or-cidr}?tld=&apex=&limit=&cursor=` | THC live (cached 6 to 24 h, stale-serve), later OpenINTEL measurements and the THC monthly dump |
| `POST /lookup/subdomains` (`domain`) → `subdomains[{subdomain, last_seen_on}]` | `GET /v1/subdomains/{domain}` | **our `names` index** (THC's live API is an auxiliary source, labelled) |
| `POST /lookup/cnames` (`target_domain`) → names | `GET /v1/cnames/{target}` | THC live; later OpenINTEL |
| `GET /download`, `/subdomains/download`, `/cnames/download` (CSV, 50,000 rows) | `GET /v1/download/{ip\|subdomains\|cnames}/{key}.csv` | streamed; no row cap other than a hard 5 M |
| Daily CT dumps (`cs2.ip.thc.org/<date>.txt.gz`, one sorted name per line, wildcards kept) | `GET /v1/dumps/names/{date}.txt.zst` (THC-compatible), `…/names-by-source/{source}/{date}` | `dumps` package, 302 to the signed URL (mirror-archlinux style: no bytes through the Worker) |
| Monthly reverse-DNS dump | link-out listing, plus `GET /v1/dumps/rdns` once Phase 4 exists | THC's URLs |

THC schemas were read from the page's own JavaScript chunks [R: thc/chunk-569.js, chunk-530.js, chunk-517.js; the OpenAPI spec itself is not served]. The rate limit is a 250-token bucket refilled at 0.5 a second per address [R: ct.md; also seen in a header on 2026-10-27: "249 requests, replenishes at 0.50/sec"]. Our Worker egress shares addresses, so `/v1/ip` and `/v1/cnames` always respond from cache first and report `x-domains-source-status` when THC refuses.

### 7.2 Our own routes

| Route | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /v1/new` | newly seen or newly registered registrable domains (or FQDNs) |
| `GET /v1/domain/{reg}` | dossier: first and last per source, `evidence[]`, name count, recent names |
| `GET /v1/name/{fqdn}` | first and last per source |
| `POST /v1/lookup` | up to 500 names or domains in one call |
| `GET /v1/suffixes?since=` | counts per suffix for a window, from daily manifest summaries |
| `GET /v1/sources`, `/v1/status`, `/v1/coverage` | source health, freshness, `corpus_since`, the cross-source table |
| `GET /v1/index/latest` | the root manifest digest and the GHCR references, so any client can read the data directly with no Worker |
| `/`, `/llms.txt`, `/llms-full.txt`, `/openapi.json`, `/SKILL.md` | generated, as other pkgforge services do |

**`/v1/new` parameters**
- `since`: `1h`, `6h`, `12h`, `1d`, `Nd` (N ≤ 31), or an ISO timestamp. Longer ranges use dumps.
- `kind`: `registrable` (default) or `fqdn`.
- `suffix`: any public suffix (`com.np`, `co.uk`, `xyz`), matched as a contiguous key range.
- `under`: a parent domain (`example.co.uk`).
- `basis`: `any`, `registered`, `zone`, `rdap`, `nrd-list`, `ct`.
- `source`: one or more source ids; `order`: `first_seen.desc` (default) or `.asc`.
- `limit` (default 1,000, max 10,000), `cursor` (opaque: tier, partition, block, key).
- `wildcards=include|only|exclude` on name routes.

**Response envelope**

### 7.3 Caching and limits
- Blocks and footers are immutable by digest: `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable`, keyed in the Cache API and R2.
- Signed blob URLs are cached for about 6 minutes (the measured signed-URL life is about 7 minutes [M11], so refresh before `se`).
- The root is cached 30 to 60 s; hot `new-*` files 60 s; THC passthrough 6 to 24 h with stale-serve.
- Single-flight per cache key inside an isolate. Reads for one query fan out over at most 6 concurrent subrequests; a cold query across about 12 tiers is two waves.
- Caps: window ≤ 31 days via API; page ≤ 10,000 rows; batch ≤ 500; CSV ≤ 5 M rows. Past that, use dumps or read GHCR directly.
- Expected cold cost, derived from my measurements (not yet measured from a Worker): root plus directory cached, then footer Range plus block Range per tier, each about 0.9 to 2.4 s from Nepal [M11]. From a Cloudflare data centre I expect lower; Phase 0 measures it.

## 8. Redundancy and failure handling

### 8.1 Behaviour when each source dies
"Core" means the corpus's content depends on it for some portion; no single source is core.

| Source dies | What the service still answers | What degrades | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **THC dumps** | all queries from Kafka and tiles | THC's 40% of Kafka's names it shares and its non-Kafka share (it was a superset of LE tiles by 97.7 to 98.1%, so LE names then come from tiles) [M9]; day-precision records stop | `sources.json` marks `thc-ct` down after a missed day; `meta.sources_failed` shows it |
| **OpenINTEL Kafka** | THC and tiles | the Google, Sectigo, DigiCert and TrustAsia coverage; the NRD topics; second-precision for non-LE names | WebSocket fallback for NRD; a bounded catch-up on return (27 h and 11 d retention) |
| **Names tiles** (a log or all) | THC and Kafka | same-day freshness for LE and Geomys; second precision | per-log health; shard rollover through the log list |
| **A names-tile log returns 404 or 429** | other logs | that log's slice | per-log backoff; availability is probed on every run [M10] |
| **NRD lists** | OpenINTEL NRD topics and CT-first | T2 evidence thins | label `basis` accordingly |
| **GHCR unreachable** | Cache API and R2 (about 3.5 GB of hot blocks and the last 48 h) | cold partitions and anything not cached | serve stale with `x-domains-source-status: ghcr-down`; `/v1/index/latest` still names digests |
| **GitHub Actions stops** (schedule drift, disabled workflows) | everything already published | freshness | workflows start each other (mirror pattern), the watchdog files an issue, the Worker's `/v1/status` shows `as_of` age |
| **Cloudflare** | clients read GHCR directly via the root manifest and the format spec | the API | the published format is the contract |
| **THC API** | `/v1/subdomains` unaffected (own index); `/v1/ip` and `/v1/cnames` serve stale, then 503 with the reason | reverse-IP and CNAME coverage | Phase 4 adds a second and third source |

### 8.2 Guardrails
- A source's segment is accepted only inside its expected rate band (tiles 2 to 3 tiles/s per log, Kafka messages per hour, THC lines per day versus the 30-day median). A THC file under 40% of the 30-day median is quarantined, not merged. THC's October volume fell to a third of September's with no explanation [M1, M2], so the band must be relative to recent history.
- A merge never removes names from a promoted tier. A bad segment is excluded and the tier is rebuilt without it.
- Rollback: the root manifest carries the previous root's digest.

## 9. Accuracy

**Validation, each run**
- Schema and sortedness of every segment; sha256 matches the annotation.
- `sld_list` from Kafka against our PSL-derived `reg`: disagreement rate published.
- Suffix sanity: names that are themselves a public suffix are rejected (4 of 38.5 M on 10-26 [M5]).
- Wildcard consistency: a wildcard and its apex are both recorded; `wild` is a flag, not a different name.

**Cross-source agreement (published daily in `coverage.json`)**: for rolling 5-minute windows per source pair, the share of A's names found in B's same-day set. This is exactly the table in §3.1, generated continuously. That table says today:
- THC against the OpenINTEL feed: 39.8% [M8].
- THC against Let's Encrypt tiles: 97.7% and 98.1% [M9].
- Sycamore against Willow: 83.5% and 81.1% [M9].
- Sycamore against the Kafka window: 71.0% [M8, M9].
The Geomys pair is not measured.

**What "newly registered" claims are worth**
- T1 (zone, RDAP) is authoritative and only available for ccTLDs with open zones (`.se .nu .ch .li .ee .sk .fr` and the 12 zone-diff TLDs) until CZDS or RDAP lanes exist. `.np` has no RDAP and no open zone (prior research), so `.com.np` can only be T2 or T3.
- T2 is a third party's claim. When OpenINTEL's `confirmed_…` topic gives a `confidence` against the RDAP date, it is passed through unchanged.
- T3 is "first certificate we saw". An older domain that never had a certificate before, or whose earlier certificates fell outside our corpus, looks new.
- Duplicated across sources, a claim from two independent families (a CT-derived feed and an NRD list) is flagged `corroborated`. Lists derived from one another (hagezi from Stamus, dontcrash from hagezi) count as one family.

**Known biases, stated**
- CT sees only domains that obtained a certificate. Domains registered and never certified are invisible to every CT-derived source.
- Shared-host suffixes (`github.io`, `blogspot`, `workers.dev`) make `site` and `reg` differ; both are stored, queries default to `reg`.
- Machine-generated cloud names (aws.dev, amazonaws.com, sfdc.net) are a large share of the Kafka-only names [M8]; they stay in the index and carry a `kind` tag later (Phase 3), and `exclude=generated` becomes a query option.

## 11. Phased plan, and what to measure first

### Phase 0, measurements only, 1 to 2 days (before any production code)
1. **Kafka from a GitHub runner**: can `ubuntu-latest` reach `kafka.zonestream.openintel.nl:9092` and `:9091`? Consume 10 minutes from `certstream_domains` with `kcat` or a Go client, confirm snappy, and compare throughput with my 59 s per 5 minutes from here.

## 12. Risks and open questions for the operator

**Decisions needed**
1. **Which sources enter the published outputs.** The source table lists each publisher's stated terms as facts. Every record has a source bitmask, so any source can be excluded from dumps and API answers mechanically. This is your call; nothing here analyses terms.
2. **Retention policy for `obs` and the `names` index** (400 days by last seen is my default). Without deletion GHCR grows about 0.5 TB a year [E]. mirror-archlinux never deletes. Do you want deletion through the API (documented as public preview [R: container-registry.md line 37]) or an indefinite archive?
3. **Is `warming` acceptable** on `new` answers for the first 90 days, with the Common Crawl baseline seed as the mitigation?
4. **Is the THC passthrough for reverse IP and CNAME acceptable as the only source until Phase 4?** The alternative is to leave those two routes out of v1.

**Risks**
- **GitHub policy change on Packages or Actions.** GHCR is free "currently", with at least a month's notice [R]. Mitigation: published format plus an offsite copy (Phase 4).
- **GitHub schedules.** Documented delays and drops, and a 60-day inactivity disable [R]. Mitigation: the mirror-archlinux watchdog pattern; commits at least monthly.
- **Kafka port.** The documented 9091 timed out and 9092 answers; either could change. WebSocket fallback exists, but without replay.
- **OpenINTEL retention is short** (27 h for names). A consumer outage over a day loses data. Hourly consumption plus a watchdog contains this.
- **THC volume shrinkage** (October vs September) means its coverage may keep falling.
- **Names-tile availability is per-log and volatile** (404 at the tail of IPng Gouda2027h1, Halloumi and GoDaddy; 429 on Geomys; 403 on HARICA) [M10].
- **Scale of the names index** is uncertain (20 to 55 GB [E]); Phase 0 step 3 fixes it.
- **Block decode CPU in Workers** is unmeasured.
- **Manual steps**: five packages must be made public by hand once; the first push of each must come from the repository's workflow.

## 13. What I could not measure

- GitHub runner reachability of Kafka, and Kafka over 9091 from anywhere (the operator machine's 9091 timed out).
- Any GHCR **write**: no large-blob push, no manifest layer-count limit, no tag limit, no untagged-version retention. I had no write access and created nothing.
- Any measurement from a Worker: cold and warm latency, CPU for block decode, which compression `DecompressionStream` accepts, the real R2 hit rate.
- Geomys's names-tile window (429 on the probe, and the window script failed).
- Whether the OpenINTEL feed is a 1:1 mirror of each log. Per-log message counts in my window did not track my log growth rates (for example DigiCert Sphinx2026h2 showed about 136 messages a second in the window against 17 an entry-second of growth at another hour), so I treat the feed as a source with unknown per-log completeness.
- The Kafka measurement topics' binary schema.
- `zone_diff` and `confirmed_newly_registered_domain` over WebSocket (zero `zone_diff` messages in 23 minutes; the confirmed topic only via Kafka offsets).
- The live THC API today (I re-read its schemas from its page chunks; the call behaviour comes from the prior research).
- Distinct-name growth beyond four days, so the year-scale index size is an estimate.
- The exact minimum interval for GitHub `schedule` (the page text was truncated in my read; the mirror runs `*/15`).
- Actions runner time per job: estimates only.
- DoH or resolution cost for any forward-DNS lane; not needed in this design, so not measured.
