# domains.pkgforge.dev: design

Draft of 2026-10-27, written read-only. It publishes CT names and newly seen/registered domains from many feeds, with our own CT reader beside them. Storage is GHCR for the bulk, an R2 cache of at most 5 GB, and Workers for serving. Claims carry evidence tags: `[M#]` means MEASURED (command and UTC time in the ledger at the end), `[R#]` means READ (URL or file), `[I]` means my inference.

## 0. The design in twelve decisions

1. **Two products, one storage engine.**
   - `names` is the per-name CT index: subdomains, suffix scans, daily dumps. It is large: about 32.5 M distinct hostnames a day from THC alone `[M2]`.
   - `domains` is the registrable-domain feed: newly seen and newly registered, first and last seen per source. It is small: hundreds of thousands of rows a day.
   - Both are immutable, sorted, block-compressed packs plus a sparse index. They sit in GHCR as OCI artifacts and are range-read by Workers.
2. **Keys are reversed-label names** (`com.example.api`), compared bytewise. Every question is then a prefix scan: subdomains of a domain, every name under `np.com`, every name under a TLD.
3. **Sources are segments.** A source is a producer of segments named `(dataset, source, tier, span)`. Removing a source removes its segments. Queries read whatever exists and report what they used. This is what makes the service provider-agnostic.
4. **The clock is split by reliability.**
   - GitHub's cron is unreliable: measured median gaps of 3.8 h on a `*/15` schedule and 5.3 h on a `*/3 h` schedule `[M8]`. Actions therefore carry only durable, idempotent, heavy work: daily THC, compaction, verification, publishing the root.
   - A Cloudflare cron Worker carries freshness: Kafka or WebSocket, ISC, names tiles. It writes a rolling 72 h hot tier into R2.
   - Hot-tier retention is longer than the worst measured GitHub gap, so a late Action delays only the cold tier.
5. **GHCR is the system of record.** A signed `root` index (tag `latest`, write-once dated tags) names every pack by repo, digest and size. A Worker reads the root, then `blobs/<digest>`, then follows the 307 to an Azure blob URL with `Range` `[M7]`. A cold read is about 2 subrequests and a warm one about 1.
6. **R2 (≤ 4.5 GB planned) holds**: the 72 h hot tier, all sparse indexes, a block cache and the own-CT Bloom filter. Everything in it is rebuildable from GHCR.
7. **Newness is defined, never implied.**
   - `seen` is evidence of appearance (SCT time or the feed's own date).
   - `claimed_new` is a feed saying "new".
   - `registered` is only stated with registry evidence (zone diff, RDAP, OpenINTEL's confirmed topic).
   - Domain-level newness is trustworthy early. Name-level newness is censored for about 200 days `[M2]`, so it is labelled.
8. **No source is complete.** On the same days, THC's CT dump contained 12–55% of ISC's new domains `[M6]`. The feed is a union with per-source provenance and a declared `confidence`, never a single answer.
9. **Reverse IP and CNAME are THC-only data** (active DNS, not CT). They are served as a labelled, cached pass-through. We do not claim to own them.
10. **Do not mirror THC's 53 GB monthly rDNS dump** (not range-readable `[R5]`). Revisit only if THC goes dark.
11. **The VPS is just another source.** It writes the same segments under its own source id with its own GHCR write identity. The triggers that justify it are measurable (section 5.6).
12. **Cost target: $0 incremental** on the existing Workers Paid plan at about 100k API requests a day. Estimated: $9–10 a month at 1 M requests a day.

## 1. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**
- Publish CT names and new/newly registered domains, with provenance per record and explicit definitions.
- Survive the loss of any one source, and answer from the rest.
- Cover THC's query types plus finer queries: any window (1 h, 6 h, 12 h, N days) filtered by any TLD, second-level suffix or parent domain, with first and last seen per name per source.
- Run entirely on free or already-paid tiers: Actions on a public repo, GHCR, Workers, ≤ 5 GB of R2.
- Be reusable by `web.pkgforge.dev`: its `domain` service reads this project's names index and new-domain feed over HTTP, or embeds the reader module.

**Non-goals**
- No VPS today. No full-CT tail (about 510 GB a day `[R5]`). No regex content-filter pipeline.
- No claim of registry truth for names merely seen in CT.
- No active DNS resolution. Reverse IP and CNAME are pass-through.
- No legal or terms analysis. Stated licences are stored as facts per source.

## 2. What the evidence says (findings that shaped the design)

### 2.1 How mirror-archlinux uses GHCR `[R1]`

I read these files in full: `README.md`, `docs/design.md`, `docs/measurements.md`, `docs/worker.md`, `worker/src/index.js`. I read headings and selected sections of `docs/operations.md`, and skimmed the workflow YAML for schedule, runner and timeout lines.

I did **not** read `scripts/*`, `client/pacman-oci`, `tests/*`, `fetchers/*`, `docs/client.md` or `docs/tests.md`.

- **Layout.**
  - One GHCR repository per target, at `ghcr.io/pkgforge-dev/mirror-archlinux/<arch>/<repo>`.
  - Each package is a tag (its escaped filename), written once. It is an OCI artifact manifest with two layers: the file and its `.sig`.
  - The repository also carries `:latest`, a dated tag, content-addressed tags (`db-<sha256>`), `:staging` and `:index`.
  - I counted 25,510 tags in `x86_64/extra` `[M7]`. Nothing is ever deleted.
- **Invariant.** `latest` moves only after everything it names is on the registry and verified. A re-run guard, a writer guard and "tag written once" rules protect it.
- **Serving.**
  - A Worker sends no bytes. It reads the manifest (anonymous, with `Authorization: Bearer QQ==`, falling back to the anonymous token flow), reads the blob's 307, and 302s the client to the signed URL.
  - It caches `loc`, `man`, `blob` and `token` entries in the Cache API. Pinned or dated manifests are cached for 1 year, `latest` for 30 s, and signed URLs for `se` minus 120 s, at most 600 s.
- **CI.**
  - Runner `ubuntu-24.04`. Sync cron `17 */3 * * *`, upstream-watch `*/15`, status hourly, watchdog daily.
  - Every job runs with `permissions: {}`, actions pinned by commit, and one `GITHUB_TOKEN`. The repository holds no secret.
  - Scheduled workflows start each other when overdue, because "GitHub can delay or drop a scheduled run". A script re-enables workflows that GitHub disabled after 60 inactive days.
- **Limits it hit.**
  - 429 `toomanyrequests` at 2,000 requests a minute per token, which bounds a seed to about 200 files a minute.
  - 10 GB per layer and a 10-minute upload.
  - A package first pushed from outside the repository is not linked, so the workflow token can never write to it.
  - A package's visibility cannot be changed by API, so a person makes it public.
  - A single push reached 34 MB/s.

### 2.2 GitHub and GHCR facts `[R2]`

- **Actions on a public repo.**
  - Standard runners are "free and unlimited": `ubuntu-latest` has 4 vCPU, 16 GB RAM and 14 GB SSD.
  - Job limit 6 h. Workflow run limit 35 days. Free-plan concurrency is 20 jobs, shared by the whole organisation. A matrix is at most 256 jobs.
  - `GITHUB_TOKEN` is limited to 1,000 REST requests an hour per repository.
- **Schedules.**
  - `schedule` "can be delayed ... some queued jobs may be dropped".
  - It runs only on the default branch, and the minimum interval is 5 minutes.
  - Scheduled workflows are disabled after 60 days with no repository activity.
- **Packages.**
  - "GitHub Packages usage is free for public packages" and "Container registry storage and bandwidth is currently free ... informed at least one month in advance of any change". That wording is a risk, not a guarantee.
  - 10 GB per layer, 10-minute upload.
- **My measurements of the mirror's own run history `[M8]`.**
  - The `*/15` cron produced 66 scheduled runs in 10.1 days against about 970 expected. The median gap was 227 min and the maximum 443 min.
  - The `17 */3` cron produced 57 runs in 12.7 days against about 101 expected. The median gap was 320 min and the maximum 512 min.
  - Once a run exists, runner start is fast: median 2 s, p90 3 s, max 7 s, over 250 jobs. Dispatch-triggered runs queue just like scheduled ones.
- **GHCR reads `[M7]`.**
  - 300 of 300 anonymous manifest GETs at concurrency 50 succeeded (median 0.45 s, p95 0.53 s, max 7.6 s). No 429 was seen at about 6 requests a second from one address.
  - Blob GETs return 307 to `pkg-containers.githubusercontent.com`, which is `Windows-Azure-Blob/1.0`, a BlockBlob.
  - The signed URL's `se` was about 8.7 minutes ahead. `Range` returned 206 in 0.57 s from Nepal.
  - 198 of 200 blob 307 requests succeeded. Two failed to connect and p95 was 15 s. This came from a multi-WAN laptop, so I treat the tail as the client's until a Worker repeats it.
  - Through the existing `forge` route, a `Range` read took 1.8–3.0 s from KTM. That path lists tags, reads the manifest and reads the blob, so it is a generic, unoptimised path.

### 2.3 Cloudflare facts `[R3]`

- **Workers Paid.**
  - Requests: 10 M a month included, then $0.30 per million. CPU: 30 M ms included, then $0.02 per million ms. No charge or limit for duration.
  - Cron invocations get 15 min wall time, and CPU defaults to 30 s (15 min for crons of an hour or longer).
  - Subrequests: 10,000 per invocation, configurable to 10 M. Six simultaneous connections waiting for headers. 128 MB memory. 250 crons per account.
- **R2.** 10 GB free, Class A 1 M and Class B 10 M a month free, egress free. This project is capped at 5 GB.

### 2.4 The ten findings

1. **THC's daily dump is bigger and messier than documented `[M2]`.**
   - The 2026-10-26 file is 319.9 MB gzipped, 1.33 GB raw, 38,474,309 lines. 9,503,378 of them (24.7%) are `*.` wildcards. 217,443 lines (0.57%) contain upper case.
   - It is not bytewise sorted: the order is a locale collation, with 10.3 M adjacent inversions. The documented "one sorted lowercase name per line" is therefore wrong, and the ingester must normalise and sort itself.
   - After lowercasing and merging wildcard rows there are 32,554,702 distinct hostnames. That is 11,162,434 ICANN-registrable domains (14,142,438 if the PSL private section is applied), across 1,002 TLDs.
   - 25 registrable domains (amazonaws.com, aws.dev, workers.dev, pages.dev, …) hold 35% of the names. 41% of registrable domains have exactly one name.
   - Files appear 00:02–00:05 UTC on D+1 `[M1]`.
   - `cs1.ip.thc.org` now 302s to `cs2` `[M1]`. The same bytes are on the Hugging Face bucket (first 20 MB identical, ranges supported). The bucket has 54 files, 35.77 GB, created 2026-10-04.
   - Daily size fell about 3× around 2026-10-03/04 (about 950 MB to about 300 MB a day). The cause is unknown.
2. **Names barely recur** `[M2]`.
   - 6.84% of 10-26's names were in 10-25's file. The figures against older days are 6.67% (10-19), 2.15% (10-12) and 1.04% (10-05).
   - 86.6% of 10-26's names were absent from the union of four earlier days (133.6 M distinct names).
   - 85.9% of 10-26's registrable domains were absent from 10-25.
   - A daily dump holds certificates issued that day. So `subdomains of github.com` is empty in it (0 names), while `np.com` has 6,764 names that day.
   - Consequence: a rolling names corpus needs about one certificate validity period (about 200 days under current rules `[I]`) before subdomain coverage is good. Responses must carry a `coverage` block, and read-through adapters fill the gap.
3. **Block density.** Names sorted in reversed-label order, front-coded and compressed per block `[M10]`:

   | names per block | deflate bytes per name | blocks per day |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1,024 | 6.26 | 31,792 |
   | 4,096 | 6.04 | 7,948 |
   | 16,384 | 5.93 | 1,987 |

   Front coding alone is 12.0 bytes per name. brotli-9 and zstd-12 land within 1.5% of deflate-9, so **deflate-raw is enough**. It is the format Workers can decode natively (standard `DecompressionStream`; I did not run it in workerd here). A daily THC pack is about 200 MB, against 320 MB for the source gzip.
4. **Zonestream's Kafka broker is anonymous and much richer than the WebSocket `[M4]`.**
   - It answers on `kafka.zonestream.openintel.nl:9092`, where ApiVersions, Metadata, ListOffsets and Fetch all worked without credentials. Port 9091, which the docs name, timed out.
   - Topics found: `newly_registered_domain`, `newly_registered_fqdn`, `confirmed_newly_registered_domain`, `certstream_domains`, `certstream`, and `*_measurements` topics.
   - Messages in the last 24 h, and retained:

     | topic | last 24 h | retained | retention |
     |---|---|---|---|
     | `newly_registered_domain` | 422,534 | 5.70 M | about 12–13 days |
     | `confirmed_newly_registered_domain` | 235,406 | 2.53 M | about 10 days |
     | `newly_registered_fqdn` | 342,918 | 3.76 M | about 11 days |
     | `certstream_domains` | 72.85 M | 74.1 M | about 25 h |

   - A server-side "offset at timestamp" query works, so an ingester is stateless apart from its stored offsets. The WebSocket is live-only, with no replay.
5. **`newly_registered_domain` is a "newly seen in CT" signal, not a registration date** `[M3,M5]`.
   - In 800 messages (180 s), `timestamp` is the emission time (rx−ts at most 1 s). `submission_timestamp` is the SCT time.
   - 61.6% of events had a certificate under 6 h old. 38% had one older than 30 days, with p90 at 144 days.
   - Against Verisign RDAP, 25 of 25 `.com`/`.net` events with a fresh certificate were registered within 1 day (median 0.1 d). Of 25 with a certificate older than 30 days, 14 were not found at all and 11 were registered 35–5,157 days earlier, so none were new registrations.
   - Rule: use the certificate age, not the emission time. The `confirmed_` topic carries `confidence` 1–10 (within 5 min up to under 24 h) `[R4]` and mixes in zone-derived rows (`ct_name: "zone_data"`, `cert_index: -1`, 9% of the sample).
6. **ISC publishes a good free new-domain feed `[M6][R4]`.**
   - `https://isc.sans.edu/feeds/domaindata.json.gz` plus dated files `domaindata.<date>.json.gz`. At least 5 prior days are kept. It is updated hourly.
   - Per day: 250,940 (10-26), 251,054 (10-25), 340,972 (10-24) and 418,062 (10-20) domains. Each file is about 2.3 MB gzipped and 28 MB as JSON.
   - Fields: `domainname`, `ip` (integer, present for about 32%), `firstseen` (a date), `score`, `scorereason`. 466 TLDs.
   - Stated terms (fact only): attribution to SANS Technology Institute / Internet Storm Center; "Do not resell".
   - Its own doc says the domains "may not be new, but new to us".
7. **WhoisDS's free daily file is a 70,000-domain sample** `[M6]`.
   - The zip for 10-26 (and 10-25, 10-24) holds exactly 70,000 domains. The WhoisDS site's own counts for September are 250k–400k a day globally. The file dated D matches ISC's day D−1: 79.8% of WhoisDS-10-26 is in ISC-10-25, 66.2% of WhoisDS-10-25 is in ISC-10-24, and about 0% for the same date. File dates are not observation dates.
   - `cenk/nrd` republishes it, but its files were last modified 2026-10-22 and hold 10 × 70k lines. It is stale and not a primary source.
8. **Cross-source agreement is low in absolute terms `[M6]`.**
   - ISC's domains first seen 10-26: 25.5% appear in THC's 10-26 dump and 54.8% in THC's 10-25 dump.
   - ISC's domains first seen 10-25: 12.4% appear in THC-26 and 22.2% in THC-25.
   - So THC's CT view holds about 12–55% of what ISC says is new. Union and provenance are the answer, not trust in one source.
9. **Our own names tiles work `[M9]`.**
   - Let's Encrypt Sycamore 2026h2 is at 1,043,000,586 entries.
   - `GET <log>/tile/names/x004/x074/216` returned 7,109 bytes gzipped (47,033 decoded) for 256 entries.
   - The body is JSONL: `{"Timestamp":ms,"Subject":{"CommonName":…},"DNS":[…]}`. Cache-Control is `public, max-age=604800, immutable`, and `Server: cloudflare`.
   - The matching data tile is 169,988 bytes gzipped, so names tiles are about 24× smaller.
   - This confirms the research's 55 M entries and about 1.4 GB a day for the Let's Encrypt, Geomys and IPng families `[R5]`. The 74% overlap between two Let's Encrypt shards is also from `[R5]`.
10. **Public certstream relays are dead**: `wss://certstream.calidog.io` accepted the connection and sent 0 messages in 12 s. `certstream.dev` is documentation for self-hosted software, not a stream `[M11]`.

## 4. Data model and definitions

**Entities**
- `name`: lowercase, IDNA/punycode, no trailing dot, LDH plus `_`. A wildcard is a flag, never part of the key.
- `suffix`: the PSL ICANN public suffix. `registrable`: suffix plus one label. A separate `site` field uses the PSL private section (workers.dev, pages.dev and similar). Every segment records `psl_version`, and compaction recomputes when it changes.
- `key`: labels reversed, joined with `.`, compared bytewise.

**Observation** (the unit every adapter writes)
```
(name|domain, source, kind, t_first, t_last, t_prec, extras{})
kind  ∈ ct_name | claimed_new | registered_claim | zone_add | list
t_prec ∈ s | h | d      (what the source can actually say)
```

**Per-name answer** `first_seen` and `last_seen` per source, plus the overall min and max. Per-source values come from the segments, not from a stored blob, so removing a source removes its contribution.

**Definitions (published in the API and openapi.json)**

| term | means |
|---|---|
| `seen` | earliest evidence the source shows for the name, at the stated precision. CT: SCT time. Dumps: the dump's UTC date (precision `d`, cutoff 00:05 UTC). Lists: the feed's own date, never the file date. |
| `first_seen` / `last_seen` | min / max of `seen` across the segments we hold. Left-censored at `corpus_since`. |
| `new_domain (seen)` | domain-level `first_seen` inside the window, over sources not marked `stale_replay`. This is the trustworthy newness. |
| `new_name` | name-level `first_seen` in the window. Valid only for windows after `corpus_since + 200 d` for the names tier. Before that it is returned with `censored:true`. |
| `claimed_new` | a source says new, with its own semantics (ISC: "new to us"). Shown with the source. |
| `registered` | only when `basis ∈ {rdap, zone, oi-confirmed}`. Never inferred from CT or lists. |

**Confidence on domain rows** (computed, always shown with its inputs)
- `registered-confirmed`: oi-confirmed `confidence ≤ 5`, or a zone add, or an RDAP creation date within the window.
- `corroborated`: at least two different `family` values saw it within ±48 h.
- `single`: one family.
- `suspect`: an `oi-nrd` event with certificate age > 7 d. Excluded from default feeds.

## 5. Ingestion pipeline

### 5.1 Where each step runs

| step | runs on | schedule | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot freshness: ISC (conditional GET), Kafka drain, optional WS, own CT names tiles | CF cron Worker | every minute for CT tiles; 5–10 min for Kafka and ISC | Writes 5–10 min journals and hourly packs to R2 `hot/`. Cursors in R2. Holds a heartbeat object. |
| THC daily names | Actions job | triggered 00:30 UTC, backed by cron | download, normalise, `LC_ALL=C sort -u`, reverse labels, encode, verify, push |
| Daily domain feed + reconcile | Actions job | after the hot tier closes the day | Merges the day's journals, runs the Kafka replay for gaps, writes `f.<source>.d.<date>` |
| Compaction | Actions job | weekly L1 for the names tier; monthly merge | merges L0 to L1 to L2 |
| Verify | same job | before any publish | section 5.5 |
| Root publish | single-writer Actions job | after each successful publish | The last step is moving `root:latest` |
| Watchdog | CF cron Worker and a daily Actions job | hourly / daily | CF checks the Actions heartbeat and dispatches or re-enables workflows. Actions checks the R2 heartbeat. Each can start the other's work. |

The dispatch from Cloudflare needs one fine-grained token with `actions:write` on this repository, held as a Worker secret by the operator. Without it, the design still works: GitHub's cron is the backstop, and the 72 h hot tier absorbs the measured delays.

### 5.2 Idempotence, dedupe, backfill

- **Segment identity.** A segment tag is deterministic: `n.thc.d.2026-10-26`. If it exists with the same content digest, skip. If the source re-publishes different bytes, the new one becomes `…-r2`, the root points to the newest revision, and an issue records the change.
- **Dedupe within a source** is `sort -u` (names) or key-merge (domains). **Across sources** it happens at read time (k-way merge) and again at compaction (L1 merges sources per month).
- **Domain ledger (own CT only).** To tell "new" from "re-issued" for names tiles, a Bloom filter of registrable domains seen is held in R2: about 720 MB at 0.1% false positives for 400 M domains `[I]`. It is OR-ed daily, and rebuilt exactly from segments monthly. Events it flags are `provisional`, and the daily reconcile makes them exact.
- **Backfill.**
  - THC: 54 days on HF (35.8 GB gzipped), about 54 × 15 runner-minutes `[I]`.
  - ISC: 5 days. WhoisDS: back-dated zips. Kafka: 12 days for the NRD topics, about 25 h for `certstream_domains`.
  - Backfill is a matrix of idempotent day jobs, at most 6 concurrent.
- **Concurrency.** Groups `root-writer` (queue, no cancel), `ingest-<source>` and `compact`. These are the mirror's group rules, because two writers must never move `latest`.

### 5.3 Publishing to GHCR

- Push packs and indexes first, then the segment manifests, then a new dated `root` tag, then retag `latest`.
- Tags are lowercase and write-once for dated and content-hash tags. A 429 costs no attempt and pauses 20–60 s with a cap, the mirror's rule `[R1]`.
- Packages are created by the workflow token so they link to the repository. The operator makes each public once. I recommend four packages: `root`, `names`, `feed`, `raw` (raw is optional, small, for replay).

### 5.4 How a new source plugs in

1. Add `sources/<id>.json`: id, family, dataset, cadence, URLs, licence statement, parser id, retention, expected volume band.
2. Implement `adapter(cursor) -> {segments, cursor', stats, errors}`. Segments are deterministic files in the common pack format. The adapter never touches the root.
3. The root builder validates a segment's annotations, grammar and counts, then lists it. An adapter that fails validation never reaches `latest`.

### 5.5 Verification before publish (the mirror's "verify" step, adapted)

- Decode the whole pack. Check bytewise sortedness, uniqueness, charset and label length, no upper case, and that counts match the annotation.
- Re-find 1,000 random names through the same reader code the Worker uses (one TypeScript module for Node and workerd).
- Compare volume with the 7-day median (±50%, or hold and open an issue `anomaly-<source>`).
- Check the PSL hash, plus the segment's `time_basis` and `t_prec` fields.
- There are no content regexes. Checks are fixed charset and length rules.

### 5.6 The VPS later

A VPS runs the same container image (encoder, verifier) and pushes `n.vps-ct.*` and `f.vps-ct.*` to the same packages from a dedicated account with write access to those packages only. The mirror warns GHCR accepts only classic tokens, which can write all packages of their owner, so use a dedicated account `[R1]`. It registers as a source (section 5.4). Nothing else in the system changes.

Triggers to build it, each measurable from `/v1/sources`:
- `thc-dump` has missed 3 consecutive days.
- `oi-nrd` and `isc-new` are both stale for more than 24 h.
- own-CT coverage stays below an agreed SLO, for example less than 60% of a validation sample's certificates.
- the operator wants full certificate-level data.

## 6. Storage layout

### 6.1 GHCR

```
ghcr.io/pkgforge-dev/domains.pkgforge.dev/
  root/    :latest  :r.<utc>  (write-once dated)         artifactType …domains.root.v1
  names/   :n.<src>.d.<YYYY-MM-DD>[-rN]   L0, one source-day
           :n.all.m.<YYYY-MM>             L1, all sources, one month
           :n.all.y.<YYYY>                L2
  feed/    :f.<src>.h.<YYYY-MM-DDtHH>     hourly events (optional on GHCR)
           :f.all.d.<YYYY-MM-DD>          daily, all sources, merged
  raw/     :isc.<date> :whoisds.<date>    small replay copies
```
Tag grammar is `[a-z0-9_][a-z0-9._-]{0,127}`, and GHCR resolves tags case-insensitively, so everything is lowercase `[R1]`. A dot in the repository name is legal.

**Manifest** (`names/n.thc.d.2026-10-26`): artifactType `application/vnd.pkgforge.domains.names.v1`; layers `names.pack` (deflate-raw blocks), `names.idx` (sparse index), optional `names.txt.gz` (plain bulk, sorted C-locale, for the most recent 60 days only); annotations: source, tier, span, `names`, bytes, `psl_version`, input ETag/digest, `anomaly`, run id, created.

**Root** (JSON, about 80 KB while it lists about 200 segments `[I]`) holds, per segment: repository, pack digest and size, idx digest and size, span, counts, source, flags. A Worker needs no per-segment manifest fetch, because blobs are scoped per repository, so the root records the repository next to each digest `[R1]`.

### 6.2 Formats

**Pack.** Blocks of N = 2,048 names by default (measured at 1,024, 4,096 and 16,384; section 2.4), each independently deflate-raw. Entry: `varint(shared_prefix) varint(suffix_len<<2 | flags) suffix_bytes`, with `flags = exact | wildcard`. Tiers L1 and up append, per record, a source mask and a first/last day offset (u16) per source. That adds an estimated 3–5 B per name, which I have not measured.

**Index.** Two levels. The top level holds the first key of every 64th index block and stays in the isolate or R2. The second level is a small range read. A one-day THC pack at 1,024 names per block has 31,792 blocks, so a flat key list would be about 1.4 MB raw `[I]`. At the default block size it is about 0.7 MB. A cold lookup costs at most 2 small range reads.

**Domain/feed records.** `{domain, source, kind, t, t_prec, confidence?, extras}`, sorted by reversed domain inside an hourly or daily pack. For hot and daily feeds the pack holds only rows a source marked new, which is 0.3–0.7 M a day across all sources `[M4,M6]`.

### 6.3 Which index answers which query

| query | structure | reads |
|---|---|---|
| names under a domain or suffix (`np.com.`) | prefix scan of reversed keys in each live names segment | one block or two per segment |
| one name | exact key per segment | idx + one block per segment |
| names first or last seen in a window | per-segment time fields (L0: the segment's date; own CT: per-record seconds) | segment filter + scan |
| new domains in last 1 h / 6 h / 12 h | hot hourly feed packs in R2 (and the latest feed packs in GHCR) | N hourly packs, prefix scan under the suffix |
| new domains in last N days | `f.all.d.*` daily feed | N daily packs, prefix scan |
| `domain` dossier | ledger (own CT) + feed events + names counts | few reads |
| bulk | the pack or the optional `names.txt.gz` blob | a 302 to GHCR's signed URL |

Fan-out for a deep-history `subdomains` query is about one segment per month plus the current month's L0 days. With weekly L1 and monthly L2 that is about 15–20 segments. At 20 reads of 6–25 KB each, that is 0.1–0.5 MB. Warm caches make this one to two cache lookups per segment; cold it is about 1–3 s `[I]`, not measured.

### 6.4 Retention

- Hot tier in R2: 72 h.
- Names L0 daily packs are pruned only after the L1 month is verified. Steady growth is about 6–7 GB a month (THC alone, 32.5 M × about 6.3 B), about 80 GB a year `[I]`. Keeping every tier would be 2–3× that. The GHCR delete API is in public preview `[R2]`, so pruning is optional.
- Domain feed packs are kept forever (about 10 MB a day).
- Operator decision: keep everything while total stays under about 200 GB (the mirror holds about 368 GiB on GHCR `[R1]`).

### 6.5 R2 budget (hard cap 5 GB, planned ≤ 4.5 GB)

| prefix | purpose | cap |
|---|---|---|
| `hot/feed/` | last 72 h of hourly feed packs and journals | 0.1 GB |
| `hot/ct/` | 48 h of own-CT hourly packs `[I]` | 0.7 GB |
| `idx/` | sparse indexes of live segments | 0.5 GB |
| `blk/` | block cache keyed by digest and range, lifecycle expiry 14 d | 2.4 GB |
| `ledger/` | the Bloom filter and its deltas | 0.7 GB |
| `root/`, `status/` | root copies, health | 0.05 GB |

The Worker stops writing `blk/` objects when a stored size counter passes 4.3 GB. Everything in R2 is rebuildable from GHCR. R2 Class A writes are budgeted under 1 M a month (about 25k a day) and Class B reads under 10 M a month. The Cache API is the first tier to protect those budgets.

## 7. Query API (Workers, `domains.pkgforge.dev`)

Routes under `/v1`. No authentication is assumed (open question 4).

| route | THC counterpart | returns |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /names?under=<domain\|suffix>&since&until&basis=first\|last&source&wildcards&limit≤1000&cursor&format=json\|ndjson\|csv\|txt` | `POST /lookup/subdomains`, `/subdomains/download` | names with per-source first/last |
| `GET /subdomains/{domain}` | the same | THC-shaped alias (`domains[{domain,last_seen_on}]`, `next_page_state` mapped to `cursor`) |
| `GET /name/{fqdn}` | — | one record: sources, first/last, registrable, suffix |
| `GET /domains/new?since=1h\|6h\|12h\|1d\|Nd\|<ISO>&until&under&suffix&basis=seen\|claimed\|registered&source&min_confidence&limit&cursor` | — | new domains |
| `GET /domains/{domain}` | — | first/last per source, claims, name counts |
| `GET /dumps`, `/dumps/names/{date}`, `/dumps/domains/{date}` | daily CT dumps, bulk | list, then 302 to GHCR |
| `GET /rdns/{ip\|cidr}`, `/cnames/{target}` | `POST /lookup`, `/lookup/cnames` | pass-through to THC live, labelled |
| `GET /sources`, `/status`, `/coverage` | — | health per source, gaps, precision stats |
| `/llms.txt`, `/openapi.json`, `/SKILL.md` | — | the sibling project's root documents |

`under` takes any suffix, second-level suffix or parent domain (`np`, `com.np`, `co.uk`, `example.com`), so the `.com.np` example generalises. `suffix` is an exact PSL-suffix match.

**Response shape**
```json
{
  "query": {"under":"com.np","since":"2026-10-27T00:00:00Z","basis":"seen"},
  "coverage": {"corpus_since":"2026-09-01","sources":{"thc-dump":{"state":"ok","through":"2026-10-26"},
               "oi-nrd":{"state":"ok","through":"2026-10-27T06:10Z"}},"note":"names issued before corpus_since appear only on renewal"},
  "count": 1000, "next": "<cursor>", "degraded": false,
  "domains": [{"domain":"example.com.np","first_seen":"2026-10-27T05:12:41Z","confidence":"single",
    "sources":{"oi-nrd":{"first":"2026-10-27T05:12:41Z","t_prec":"s","kind":"claimed_new","cert_age_s":4}}}]
}
```

- **Pagination** is keyset: the cursor is the last key, merged across segments.
- **Caching.**
  - Immutable blocks (`digest+offset`): 1 year.
  - Root: 30 s. Current-hour feed: 30 s. Closed hours and days: 1 day.
  - Query responses use `s-maxage` = the minimum TTL of their inputs.
- **Provenance headers:** `x-domains-segments`, `x-domains-served-from: cache|r2|ghcr`, `x-domains-degraded`, `x-domains-sources-used`. Pass-through responses carry `source: thc-live`. We never report a capability a path did not use.
- **Limits.**
  - At most 1,000 rows a page (JSON) or 100,000 (streamed ndjson/csv).
  - At most 40 segments a query.
  - Cron Worker CPU is configured explicitly (default 30 s for under-hour crons).
  - THC's CSV cap of 50,000 rows is matched or exceeded.
  - Request and CPU costs are in section 10.

## 8. Redundancy and failure handling

| what dies | detection | the service then | recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| THC cs2 | HEAD or file missing by 06:00 UTC | HF bucket, then the cs1 alias. If all fail, mark a gap. `/names` answers from `ownct` and others. | Backfill when it returns (25 d on cs2, 54 on HF). Issue `stale-thc-dump` after 36 h. |
| THC content shift | volume outside ±50% of the 7-day median | publish with `anomaly:true`, exclude from "new" | operator decides |
| THC live API | 429 or 5xx | 503 `provider_unavailable`; cached copy ≤ 24 h, then stale-labelled | none |
| OpenINTEL Kafka | no new offsets 15 min | WS fallback; family `oi` marked stale, others continue | replay from stored offset (about 12 d for NRD, about 25 h for `certstream_domains`) |
| ISC | stale ETag 6 h, or no file by 03:00 | stale marker, continue | 5-day backfill |
| WhoisDS | 404 | stale marker | back-dated zip |
| a CT log | 429, 5xx, 404 | other logs continue; cursor stays | log list refreshed daily |
| GHCR slow or down | Worker 5xx or timeout > 10 s | R2 blocks and Cache API; hot tier unaffected; `degraded:true` | retry with the mirror's 429-pause rule |
| R2 down | R2 errors | read GHCR directly; windows under 72 h answered from GHCR feed packs (stale by up to an hour) | none |
| Cloudflare | outage | clients use GHCR directly (`root:latest` is anonymously readable); README shows the `curl` commands | none |
| GitHub Actions stopped or disabled (60 days) | CF watchdog compares the Actions heartbeat | hot tier continues 72 h; CF re-enables workflows through the API | re-run idempotent day jobs |
| CF cron stopped | Actions watchdog compares the R2 heartbeat | the Actions backup path drains Kafka and ISC | restart the cron |

Each scheduler watches the other, as in the mirror `[R1]`. Every source adapter fails alone: it never blocks another source's segments or the root.

## 9. Accuracy

- **What a record is allowed to say.**
  - CT-derived rows say `seen` and never `registered`.
  - Feed rows keep the feed's own date. Corrections applied on ingest: WhoisDS file date minus 1 `[M6]`; Zonestream uses `submission_timestamp`, and marks events whose certificate is over 7 days old as `suspect`.
- **Left-censoring.** `corpus_since` is per source and per dataset, with a flag on every name first seen within `corpus_since + 200 d` `[M2,I]`. Domain-level newness, backed by feeds with their own history (ISC, OpenINTEL), is not censored this way.
- **Cross-source agreement** (published daily in `/status`): precision proxies using the pairs measured today. WhoisDS-D vs ISC-(D−1) was 79.8% / 66.2%. THC vs ISC on the same days was 12–55%. These become time series.
- **Validation sample.** Daily, 200 random `claimed_new` rows with an RDAP-capable TLD are checked against the registry's `registration` event, per source, with Verisign for `.com` and `.net` and IANA bootstrap servers elsewhere (rate-limited; Google Registry answered 429 in earlier research `[R5]`). One-off result today: 25 of 25 fresh-certificate Zonestream events registered within 1 day; 0 of 25 old-certificate events were new `[M5]`. The sample is small and `.com`/`.net` only.
- **Coverage statement.** Responses name the sources used and their `through` time. THC's own ct.md notes it lacks about 51 real CT names for github.com while holding 4,293 others `[R5]`. We keep all names and add flags, rather than THC-style regex filtering.
- **Machine-generated spaces.** 25 registrable domains hold 35% of the names `[M2]`. A heavy domain is paginated, not dropped.

## 12. Risks and open questions for the operator

1. **GHCR is free "currently"**, with a promised one-month notice `[R2]`. Mitigation: portable blobs, an optional mirror of the root and recent days (for example a Hugging Face dataset), and a retention cap. Which do you want?
2. **GitHub Actions can be late or stopped** `[M8]`. Does the operator accept holding one fine-grained `actions:write` token as a Cloudflare Worker secret for the watchdog and dispatch? Without it, GitHub's cron is the only trigger, and the hot tier covers 72 h of slack.
3. **THC is the only large names source.** Its volume dropped about 3× in early October for unknown reasons. Do you want a standing contact or notification route?
4. **Consumer limits.** The sibling project ruled that consumers are never gated. Does the same hold here, given an open GHCR/R2 read path behind a Worker? Cloudflare's own abuse controls would still apply.
5. **Per-source stated licences** differ (OpenINTEL: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; ISC: attribution, "do not resell"; the repository is 0BSD). Segments store the stated licence as a field. You decide whether any source's packs are excluded from public bulk. I make no analysis.
6. **Retention**: keep everything (about 80 GB a year for names, 2–3× if no pruning), or roll off after N days? And is pruning through the GHCR delete API acceptable?
7. **Name-level newness** needs about 200 days of corpus. Is shipping `new_name` as "censored" until then acceptable, or should it be withheld?
8. **Hosting a plain-text bulk dump** (`names.txt.gz`, about 260 MB a day) alongside the pack doubles storage for those days. Opt in?
9. **Naming and packages.** Four GHCR packages under `pkgforge-dev`, each made public by hand once. OK?

## 13. What I could not measure

- Everything on the Worker side: GHCR range-read latency from a Worker, `DecompressionStream` CPU, GHCR rate limits at Worker scale, Cloudflare cron reliability, `connect()` to Kafka, R2 and Cache API behaviour under this workload.

- Kafka: Snappy decode, consumer-group offset commits, sustained throughput. The `certstream_domains` payload was only partially read (`{"certIndex":…,"domain_list":[…]}`).
- Names novelty beyond four older days (lags 1, 7, 14, 21 only), and THC's coverage against full CT (only against ISC).
- Own-CT overlap with THC (one tile read); per-tier L1/L2 densities; index sizes in practice.
- OpenINTEL ccTLD lists (terms redirect), AXFR (port 53), CZDS, DNS Coffee and xReverseLabs (keys or login), ISC and WhoisDS publication times of day, and why WhoisDS caps the free file at 70,000.
- RDAP validation covered only `.com`/`.net` at n = 25 + 25, and IDN handling (some list files carry Unicode names, e.g. cenk's, which must be punycoded and validated).
